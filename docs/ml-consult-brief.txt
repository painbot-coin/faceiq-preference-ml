FACEIQ ATTRACTIVENESS SCORING - CONSULTING BRIEF
2026-08-04

IN ONE BREATH
We score attractiveness from pairwise "which face wins" comparisons, not 1-10 ratings.
~52k pairs are labelled by a VLM; ~10k of those also have paid human panel votes mixed in.
A Bradley-Terry fit turns those comparisons into a latent strength (theta) per cohort face.
A siamese neural comparator is trained on the same pairs so it can judge new uploads.
At inference we place an upload against ~200 known-theta references (1-D MLE), then map to
a /10 score, a 7-tier band, and a crowd-agreement sentence via a fitted temperature T.


GOAL
Give someone who uploads a photo an attractiveness rating that is defensible: accurate,
calibrated, and honest about its own uncertainty. It replaces a hand-tuned geometric
formula that had no ground truth behind it and no way to know when it was wrong.


1. WHY PAIRWISE, NOT RATINGS
Our ground truth is gathered from A/B (pairwise) match ups. Asking people to rate a face 1-10 conflates where the face sits with where that rater's
personal zero sits, and two honest raters differ by a constant nobody can measure. "Which
of these two is more attractive" needs no shared scale. So we collect only comparisons and
recover a scale afterwards. This drives the whole architecture. 


2. THE DATA
- 3,000 faces from our app's uploads, stratified across the old formula's deciles.
- ~52,500 same-gender pairs labelled by a VLM (gemini-2.5-flash, fixed prompt). Human audit
  on a 750-pair seeded sample: 84.9% agreement. 47,914 pairs survive QC into the fit.
- Four rounds of paid human panel labelling on Prolific: 963 raters, 95,245 votes on 10,280 of
  those pairs, $4,155. Runs 1-3 targeted near-ties; run 4 was a uniform random draw, which
  is what made unbiased evaluation possible.
Same-gender only, so the two comparison graphs are disconnected and every scale is
per-gender.


3. BRADLEY-TERRY
Each face gets a latent strength theta, P(i beats j) = sigmoid(theta_i - theta_j), all
thetas fit jointly by MLE over ~48k comparisons. Global solve: a face's score is informed
by faces it never met, through chains of shared opponents.

Gates we hold it to: graph connected, no face under 15 resolved comparisons, Spearman >
0.95 between the full fit and an 80% subsample.

Theta is identified only up to shift and scale, so a pre-registered monotone curve maps
percentile -> /10. That curve is a product decision, not a measurement, and it is
deliberately the one place "what counts as a 7" is defined.

Resolution, measured: the median face's 95% rank interval is 237 of ~1,422 places, and two
faces must be ~210 places apart to be statistically distinguishable. 0.00% of adjacent
pairs are distinguishable. This is the most important number in the project and it is why
we ship bands and tiers, not decimals.


4. THE NEURAL COMPARATOR
BT cannot score an unseen face, so we train a siamese net (ArcFace R50, 112px, shared-weight
scorer, pairwise logistic loss) on (photoA, photoB, winner). Split by face id, never by
pair. Best model sees 33,449 training pairs.

It beats BT on near-tie pairs and loses to BT by 1.8-3.1 points on the global ordering, the
deficit concentrated in the 10-45 percentile-gap band. Five arms against this - more labels,
soft vs hard targets, human-grounded checkpoint selection, a variance head, 4x resolution -
all tied. A run that saw no human labels loses the same bands. We read it as structural: BT
solves a global system, the comparator only ever sees local pairs.


5. WHAT WE SHIP - REFERENCE-SET PLACEMENT
The comparator's raw scalar is unanchored by construction (only differences appear in the
loss), so we use it the way it was trained.

Precomputed: ~200 cohort faces per gender, tier-stratified, each with known theta and a
cached comparator score. Per upload: crop -> one forward pass -> compare against all 200
reference scores (signs only) -> fit the theta that explains those 200 outcomes ->
percentile against the full cohort -> /10 -> tier and band.

That fit is a 1-D conditional MLE. With p_j = sigmoid(theta - theta_j), the log-likelihood
derivative is sum_j (y_j - p_j), so the answer is the theta at which predicted wins equal
observed wins. Second derivative -sum_j p_j(1-p_j) is strictly negative, so Newton converges
in a few steps and the curvature gives se(theta) = 1/sqrt(sum_j p_j(1-p_j)) free.

Two consequences: each reference's weight is p_j(1-p_j), max 0.25 for an even matchup and
near zero for a mismatch, so the set self-selects toward faces near the user and curating it
is pointless. And a face beating every reference has no finite solution - we cap and flag.

Held-out performance: median error 0.34 / 0.28 /10 (F/M), p90 0.93, tier-exact 67%,
Spearman 0.926 against the ranking of record.


6. TEMPERATURE - HOW UNCERTAINTY BECOMES A PRODUCT
Run 4's uniform draw gave us, for thousands of pairs, both the /10 gap between the two faces
and the fraction of raters who picked the higher-scoring one. Fit p = sigmoid(gap / T) over
all of them. One free parameter. T = 1.920.

T is the bluntness of the scale: how far apart two faces must be before people reliably
agree. It transfers to a new user because it is a property of the scale, not of any pair -
once someone is placed at 6.4, the gap to anything else is a number on that same scale.

Consequences: 2-in-3 agreement needs a 1.33 point gap, 3-in-4 needs 2.11, 90% needs 4.22. A
1-10 scale holds about seven 1.33-wide bands, and the rank-resolution number independently
gives ~7 (1430/210) - two unrelated routes to the same answer, so we ship 7 tiers. Tier
width is 9/7 = 1.286 against T*ln2 = 1.331, a ratio of 1.035, so "two thirds of people would
place you above the tier below you" is almost exactly the scale's natural unit. That is the
default user-facing sentence.


7. THE NUMBER THAT MATTERS
Full production path (comparator -> placement -> /10) on run 4's uniform pairs, 2,370 pairs:

  predicting which face a CROWD prefers:    81.9%   vs 73.2% for a typical individual rater
  matching one RANDOM person's opinion:     70.8%   vs 70.4% for another random person

Both ceilings are one held-out rater against the majority of the others, which is the only
honest comparator - 100% is unreachable because people disagree. Row one is the real claim:
the system beats an individual human at anticipating group preference. Row two is a tie, and
a tie is the correct outcome there.


8. WHY WE STOPPED BUYING DATA
Headroom analysis said $7,463 more labelling was justified - purchased votes beat the free
VLM label on pairs under a 20-percentile gap, all intervals clear of zero. We then tested
whether that quality reaches a user's score. Both routes are shut:

- Through the reference set: hold the comparator fixed, swap the ranking supplying reference
  theta from zero human votes to all 95,245. Accuracy moves 0.2 points, downward. Mechanism
  is the p_j(1-p_j) weighting - reference errors average out over 200 comparisons.
- Through the comparator's training set: best label recipe at best data volume. Dead tie,
  +0.00 pts, 95% CI [-0.96, +0.96], paired bootstrap over 2,390 pairs.

Label quality is no longer the binding constraint. We also nearly shipped a composite with
the old geometric formula that improved agreement with BT by 11% (significant, replicated)
while being flat-to-worse against actual human votes - BT is a proxy fitted on VLM labels,
and the blend was fitting the proxy's error. Standing rule now: nothing counts unless it is
scored against panel votes.


9. WHAT WE WANT FROM YOU
Direction plus a math audit. Specifically:

a) Is reference-set placement the right way to put an unseen face on a BT scale, or is there
   something better-founded? We know it is a conditional MLE holding the graph fixed, and
   that a full refit would move reference thetas by ~0.4%.
b) The comparator's global-ordering deficit. We concluded structural after five failed arms.
   Right call, or is there an obvious objective/architecture we skipped - listwise or ranking
   losses, distillation from BT theta, something else?
c) Is the temperature fit sound, and is one global T defensible, or should it vary with
   position on the scale?
d) Are the uncertainty numbers honest? se from Fisher information, the 237-place rank
   interval, and how we combine estimation and disagreement variance into one band.
e) Is the "beats an individual human" claim stated correctly given two metrics with
   different ceilings?
f) Strategically: is this better than a well-engineered deterministic formula, now that we
   have ground truth to benchmark against? The old formula alone scores 71.7% against the
   73.2% human ceiling, which is closer than we expected.
g) Anything unoptimised or obviously leaving accuracy on the table.

OPEN AND UNMEASURED
Everything above is measured on faces from the same 3,000. We are about to validate on faces
from outside that pool - that is the one untested link. We also never collected test-retest
data (same person, two photos), so per-person score stability is unknown and multi-photo
band narrowing is unbuildable until we do.
